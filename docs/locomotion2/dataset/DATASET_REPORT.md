# InterPet4D Dataset Report (locomotion2)

Generated by `inspect_interpet4d.py`. Source: [InterPet4D](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ohicarip/interpet4d) / [arXiv:2607.10287](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10287), locally cloned at `dataset/interpet4d/`. This is locomotion2 scope only; locomotion1 (`docs/walk_ref`, the quality-gait campaign) is untouched.

## 1. File structure

- `pet_npy/`: 227 `.npy` files + `norm_stats.json`
- `smal_npy/`: 226 `.npz` files
- Clips present in **both**: 226
- Clips with **pet_npy only** (no SMAL fit): 1 -> ['interpet_dog01_p01_take01_ego_002']
- Clips with **smal_npy only**: 0
- Frame-count mismatch between pet_npy and smal_npy for the same clip id: 150/225 clips differ (usually pet has 1 more frame than smal, but sometimes tens more -- e.g. dropped/occluded frames during SMAL fitting). **pet_npy carries no frame index of its own**, so its rows cannot be reliably matched frame-for-frame to smal_npy's `frame_idx` when counts differ. All position/timing analysis (speed, straightness) therefore uses `smal_npy`'s `t_world` + `frame_idx` exclusively. `pet_npy` is used for two things that don't need frame-exact alignment: its own aggregate detection-confidence score, and gait-cycle timing (Section 8) -- its keypoints have no SMAL temporal-smoothing prior, which turned out to matter (see Section 8).

Naming convention: `interpet_dog{DD}_p{PP}_take{TT}_ego_{NNN}` -- dog ID, human participant ID, take number within that dog+person pairing, and clip index within the take (a take can be split into multiple short clips, hence `_ego_002`, `_ego_003`, ...). `ego` marks these as the egocentric-view derived tracks (the paper also records multi-view + audio, not present in this local `pet_npy`/`smal_npy` subset).

- **1 clips failed to load/analyze:**
  - `interpet_dog06_p14_take01_ego_001`: ValueError('SMAL fit contains non-finite (NaN/inf) values in at least one frame -- excluded from scoring')

## 2. Array shapes and dtypes

`pet_npy/<clip>.npy`: single array, shape `(T, 20, 4)`, `float32`.
- Axes 0-2 of the last dim: keypoint `(x, y, z)`.
- Axis 3: a per-keypoint confidence/score in observed range `[0.0, ~0.95]` (matches the HF dataset card's description of `(x, y, z, score)`).

`smal_npy/<clip>.npz`: dict of arrays, all length `T` in the first axis:

| key | shape | dtype | meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `pose_rotmat` | `(T, 35, 3, 3)` | float32 | per-joint rotation matrices, SMAL pose (35 joints) |
| `betas` | `(T, 30)` | float32 | SMAL shape coefficients -- verified constant within a clip (std < 5e-6) |
| `betas_limbs` | `(T, 7)` | float32 | additional limb-length shape coefficients -- also constant within a clip |
| `R_world` | `(T, 3, 3)` | float32 | per-frame global rotation of the fitted mesh into world coords |
| `t_world` | `(T, 3)` | float32 | per-frame global root translation (world coords, meters) |
| `s_world` | `(T,)` | float64 | per-frame global scale of the fitted mesh |
| `kp_world` | `(T, 24, 3)` | float32 | 24 world-frame keypoints derived from the fitted SMAL mesh |
| `kp_weight` | `(T, 24)` | float32 | per-keypoint fitting confidence/weight in `[0, ~0.95]` |
| `frame_idx` | `(T,)` | int32 | index into the native 60 Hz capture timeline (see FPS section) |

`betas`/`betas_limbs` being constant per clip confirms they encode this dog's fixed body shape, not per-frame pose.

## 3. FPS / timestamps

No FPS field ships with either array. Per the InterPet4D paper (arXiv:2607.10287), the capture rig is 12 wire-synced GoPro Hero13 cameras at **60 fps**, and the paper states motion is trained at 30 fps after downsampling. Locally, `smal_npy`'s `frame_idx` is consistent with exactly this: it indexes into the 60 Hz timeline and its consecutive gaps are almost always 2 (i.e. ~30 Hz effective), with occasional larger gaps where a frame's SMAL fit was evidently dropped (occlusion / failed fit). **We therefore compute every per-step `dt` as `diff(frame_idx) / 60.0` rather than assuming a fixed frame rate** -- this matters because gap sizes vary per clip (see `dropped_frame_ratio` below).

`pet_npy` ships no timestamp or frame-index field at all. Its row spacing was checked indirectly: for 30 sampled clips, `n_pet_frames / smal_derived_duration_s` clusters tightly at **~30.0-30.3 Hz** (std 0.52 Hz) -- consistent with the paper's 30 fps training rate and with smal_npy's own ~30 Hz effective sampling. `PET_ASSUMED_FPS = 30.0` in this script rests on that check, not on a documented field.

## 4. Dog keypoint definition (pet_npy, 20 keypoints)

No label file ships with `pet_npy`. Per the InterPet4D paper, the raw pet skeleton is a 20-point AnimalPose-style set: `L_Eye, R_Eye, L_EarBase, R_EarBase, Nose, Throat, TailBase, Withers` (8 body/head points) plus 4 legs x `{Knee, Elbow, Paw}` (12 leg points) = 20. **The paper does not give the array index order, and no local file confirms it** -- so this script does not trust an assumed name-to-index mapping. Instead, `analyze_clip` identifies probable "paw" indices in `pet_npy` itself, per clip: the 4 keypoints with the largest excursion relative to the per-frame centroid of all 20 points, and the lowest average relative height (centroid used as a stand-in for a root point, since pet_npy has none). This is a geometric heuristic, not a verified anatomical label -- but it is corroborated across clips: in a 15-clip spot check spanning 7 different dogs, the selected indices consistently clustered in **{14, 15, 16, 17, 18, 19}**, i.e. a stable 6-index subset of the 20, consistent with these being the dataset's 4 paw keypoints (plus their two adjacent knee joints, which move similarly). Treat `paw_candidate_idxs` in the per-clip metrics as "probable paw-like points," not ground truth.

## 5. SMAL representation

Standard SMAL parametric dog body model: 30 PCA shape coefficients (`betas`) plus 7 additional limb-length coefficients (`betas_limbs`) define the rest-pose mesh/skeleton; 35 joint rotations (`pose_rotmat`, as 3x3 matrices rather than axis-angle) pose it per frame; `R_world`/`t_world`/`s_world` place the posed mesh into a shared world frame per frame; `kp_world` are 24 keypoints read off that posed, placed mesh, with `kp_weight` giving the fitter's per-keypoint confidence for that frame. The paper does not document the exact vertex/joint regressor behind the 24 `kp_world` points, and none of the local files name them, so, as above, we identify likely paw points empirically rather than by index.

Empirical check that axis **2** (0-indexed, i.e. `z`) is "up": across 40 sampled clips, the per-axis mean std-dev of `t_world` (root translation) over each clip is `[0.2253, 0.3387, 0.0788]` (x,y,z) -- a walking dog's root height should vary far less than its ground-plane position, and axis 2 has the smallest variance by a clear margin. This script uses that axis for height/gait analysis and the other two as the ground plane for speed/straightness. The same check against `pet_npy`'s per-frame 20-keypoint centroid (no root point to use directly) agrees: axis 2 again has the smallest std (`[0.2041, 0.3149, 0.0419]` (x,y,z) over 30 clips), so both files use the same axis convention.

## 7. Sequence / interaction labels

None ship locally -- no per-clip activity/interaction label file exists in `dataset/interpet4d/`. The paper describes four interaction categories at the dataset level (Petting, Commanding, Calling, Free-form -- the last including fetch/tug-of-war/chase), but does not provide a clip-to-category mapping we can read from these files. "Natural walking" clips are therefore found by direct motion analysis (root speed, path straightness, gait-cycle periodicity), not by label lookup, as requested.

## 8. Walking-sequence search methodology

Analyzed 225 clips (of 226 with both modalities; 1 failed to load or were excluded, e.g. for containing non-finite SMAL-fit values -- see Section 1). Position/timing metrics use `smal_npy`; gait-cycle timing uses `pet_npy` (see below for why):

- **Speed**: per-step ground-plane speed from `smal_npy`'s `t_world`, using the real `frame_idx` gap for `dt` at each step (not a fixed fps).
- **Straightness**: net displacement / total path length over the clip (1.0 = perfectly straight; low values mean pacing, circling, or standing and turning).
- **Gait cycles**: first attempted via autocorrelation of the 4 probable-paw keypoints' root-relative height in `smal_npy`'s `kp_world`. That produced an identical ~0.233s dominant period in every clip regardless of forward speed (checked across 25 sampled clips spanning 0.07-0.73 m/s; correlation between speed and detected period was 2e-16) -- a clear sign it was picking up a fixed artifact of the SMAL fit's temporal smoothing prior, not real gait. Switched to `pet_npy`'s raw keypoints (no such prior): the same 25-clip check then showed periods varying 0.71-1.93s clip-to-clip, and consistently implicating paw-like keypoints (Section 4). Final method: FFT power spectrum of the mean root-relative height of the 4 probable-paw `pet_npy` keypoints, dominant frequency restricted to a 0.3-2.5s period band, accepted only if its power is >= 2.5x the band's median power (otherwise treated as non-periodic, 0 cycles). Cycle count = clip duration (from `smal_npy`'s `frame_idx`) / detected period.
- **Fit stability / occlusion proxies** (no human-pose data ships in this subset, so these stand in for "minimal human obstruction/contact"): fraction of frame_idx gaps larger than the clip's modal gap (`dropped_frame_ratio`, a proxy for the fitter losing the dog, often from occlusion), coefficient of variation of `s_world` (`scale_jitter_cv`, a proxy for fit instability), and mean `kp_weight` / mean `pet_npy` confidence.
- **Size preference**: bonus from the Section 6 dog-size ranking, favoring smaller dogs.

A clip is **rejected** (hard filter) if: duration < 4.0s; mean speed outside the dataset-derived "low/moderate" band `[0.199, 0.420] m/s` (the 20th-70th percentile of mean clip speed across the whole dataset -- chosen from the data rather than an arbitrary constant, since we don't have a labeled walking-speed reference); 90th-percentile per-step speed > 2.0x the band's high end (a sustained burst -- using the 90th percentile rather than the raw max because raw per-step max speed is dominated by single-frame SMAL-fit jitter in this dataset: its dataset-wide median is ~1.9 m/s, i.e. essentially every clip has at least one noisy spike, making it useless as a filter on its own); straightness < 0.35; fewer than 3 detected gait cycles; dropped-frame ratio > 0.35; or mean `kp_weight` < 0.15. Passing clips are ranked by a weighted composite `walk_score` (speed-band centering 20%, straightness 20%, gait-cycle count 20%, SMAL fit confidence 15%, raw detector confidence 5%, scale stability 10%, frame-drop cleanliness 5%, dog-size preference 5%).

11/225 clips passed every hard filter; 214 were rejected. Top rejection reasons across all rejected clips:

| rejection reason | # clips |
|---|---|
| low path straightness | 203 |
| mean speed outside walking band | 113 |
| sustained speed burst (p90 step speed) | 109 |
| fewer than 3 gait cycles detected | 1 |
